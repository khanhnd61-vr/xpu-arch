// hierarchy

One ladder, from the package to a single lane

CPUs and GPUs are built as the same kind of nest. Units are grouped into clusters, clusters share a cache, and at the bottom sits a register that holds a few 32-bit lanes. They differ in where the width is: a CPU core runs a few threads, each with wide registers, while a GPU runs thousands of narrow threads and switches between them to hide memory latency. // registers

Where the ladder ends: the register file

vla.simd's micro-kernel keeps one output tile of every matrix product in vector registers. AVX2 and NEON each give it 512 bytes of architectural registers, cut in different ways, and that alone sets the tile shape. A GPU partition has 128 times as much register storage, because it keeps dozens of threads resident at once.

AVX2Raptor Lake · Alder Lake · Zen 3
6 × 166 rows × 2 registers × 8 lanes

12 + 2 + 1 = 15 of 16 ymm registers

Sixteen 256-bit registers. Twelve accumulators hold the tile, two hold a row of weights, and one holds a broadcast activation that is reused down the whole tile.

NEONCortex-A76 · Apple M4
4 × 164 rows × 4 registers × 4 lanes

16 + 4 + 4 = 24 of 32 v registers

Thirty-two 128-bit registers, half as wide. A row needs four registers, and each row reads its activations lane by lane from its own vector, so a taller tile would spill.

CUDAAmpere · Blackwell SM partition
64 KBper partition, 256 KB per SM
16,384 × 32-bitshared by every warp resident on the partition

up to 255 registers per thread

The register file is the largest on-chip memory of an SM. A warp that stalls on memory is swapped for another whose registers are already loaded, so latency is hidden by switching between warps, not by holding a tile.

Counts are architectural registers. Cores rename them onto larger physical files, which the kernel cannot address. Colours: accumulators, weights, broadcast or laned activations, free. Tiles and register budgets are from the vla.simd paper.

How the diagrams were drawn

What a diagram shows

Each diagram is a logical map: which units exist, how they are grouped, and which caches each group shares. It is not a floorplan, and block sizes are not to scale.

Units that exist on the die but are disabled in this product are dashed. Which SMs or cores are fused off varies from chip to chip, so they are drawn at the end of a row.

Where the numbers come from

From vendor datasheets, product pages and architecture whitepapers. Where vendors publish no figure, the source is a die analysis or a measurement by Chips and Cheese and similar sites. Each device links its sources.

Bandwidth figures that no vendor publishes are calculated from bus width and data rate. Where sources disagree, the card says so.

What “measured” means

A ringed frame is a processor that has rows on VLA Hub: vla.cpp through ggml backends (CUDA, Metal, SYCL, OpenVINO, Hexagon, CPU) or vla.simd through its own AVX2 and NEON kernels.

The register tiles come from the vla.simd paper, which chooses them per instruction set.
